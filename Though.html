Though is a word in the English language used to introduce a contrast, acknowledge an opposing point, or add a qualification to a statement. In the CLVBHOUSE Wiki, the word is also associated with the mysterious Thoughjak, which has appeared throughout the article for reasons that remain unclear.

Meaning

Though usually means something similar to “although” or “even if”. It introduces information that contrasts with what has already been said. Though a statement may sound convincing, a contrasting fact can still be true.

The word can appear at the beginning, in the middle, or at the end of a sentence. Its meaning depends on how it is used.

Wiki note: Thoughjak is not required by English grammar, though it seems to have been required by whoever edited this page.

Grammar and usage

When used as a conjunction, though connects two ideas that contrast. For example: “Though it was raining, the match continued.” The rain and the continuation of the match form the contrast.

At the end of a sentence, though can add a qualification: “That was a good film, though.”

Although is often interchangeable with though, but the two words do not always fit naturally in exactly the same positions.

Examples

  • Though the room was small, it was comfortable.
  • The task was difficult. It was enjoyable, though.
  • Though Thoughjak appeared, nobody questioned it.
  • The diamond was cyan, though the emerald remained green.
