Soyspeak ('speak), occasionally known as Jakspeak, is a partially constructed international auxiliary language used primarily within the online community of soyjak.party and formerly associated with /qa/ on 4chan.

It is based primarily on English, a Germanic language belonging to the Indo-European language family. Soyspeak is characterised by deliberate spelling changes, apostrophisation, specialised vocabulary, and a parallel Greek-letter writing system.

Descriptions of the language sometimes jokingly characterise it as a convergence of Hebrew and Uzbek. Its development and conventions are closely tied to the internet communities in which it is used.

History

According to the community's lore, Soyspeak originated from a set of codes created by a figure known as Patient 5202, formerly called Dr. Soyowicz. In this account, the codes were developed for patients of the so-called Mental Asylum to communicate secretly.

The same story connects the language to espionage surrounding the Great Asylum Insurrection of 2020. These details belong to the community's narrative and should not be understood as independently verified historical events.

Outside that origin story, Soyspeak has continued to evolve through everyday use, memes, repeated expressions, and the language habits of its online speakers. It has also inspired related forms, including Sprec-Nu, also described as Proper Soyspeak.

Alphabet

Soyspeak is written using both the Latin alphabet and an adapted Greek-letter system. The Greek version is described as having 27 letters or letter combinations, including a distinct representation for th. The alternate writing system is sometimes used to get around text filters.

Apostrophisation

Apostrophisation ('postrophisation), also known as contraction ('traction), is a central feature of Soyspeak grammar. It generally involves removing the first syllable of a word and replacing it with an apostrophe.

The rules are not entirely consistent: the resulting word must remain understandable, and practice varies by speaker and dialect.

English phrase Standard Jakspeakian example Illustration of over-contraction
To my house we shall go 'o my 'ouse we/ve 'hall go 'o 'y 'ouse 'e 'hall 'o
I am going to kill you I am 'oing 'o 'ill you/xou ' a' 'oing to 'ill 'ou
Note: The examples illustrate community conventions, not a universal standard. Some words are intentionally left unchanged for clarity.

Exceptions

Words shorter than three characters, such as I, do, and a, are generally not apostrophised. A word may also remain intact when shortening it would make it indistinguishable from another word.

Two frequently cited exceptions are:

  • To becomes 'o, while do remains unchanged.
  • Be becomes 'e, while me remains unchanged.

The convention is sometimes explained as having developed during periods when online communities used shortened words to avoid automated word filters.

Other forms

Sprec-Nu

Sprec-Nu, also called Proper Soyspeak, is a related form associated with more formalised language conventions.

NuSpeek

NuSpeek is described in community references as a variation of Soyspeak. Its conventions may differ from those of other forms.

Community and usage

Soyspeak functions as a marker of familiarity with the culture, expressions, and humour of the communities where it is used. Speakers may use it in posts, replies, memes, and other online communication.

Outside those communities, the language is sometimes characterised as coded slang associated with the political tone or controversial content of some forums. Such characterisations reflect particular viewpoints and should not be treated as a complete description of every speaker or use of the language.
